Tier 1 — the daily bread

Without these you're vibing, not measuring.

Dot product, norms, cosine similarity

Every shift vector Δ = z(dirty) − z(clean); every angle claim is cos θ = ⟨a,b⟩/(‖a‖‖b‖). Cosine ignores magnitude — know when that's a feature vs a bug. → analysis/geometry.py
  • video3B1B — "Dot products and duality" (Ch. 9 of Essence of Linear Algebra)~12 min Dot product as projection — the geometric picture behind every cosine you'll compute.
  • interactiveImmersive Linear Algebra — Ch. 3, The Dot Product~1 h Drag-the-vectors illustrations; projection and angle you can feel.

Projection & orthogonality

The correction "wheel" = project onto a distortion axis and subtract. Gate 1's whole point: is severity info orthogonal to loudness, or just loudness again? → probes/run.py (energy control)
  • video3B1B — "Inverse matrices, column space and null space" (Ch. 7)~12 min Column space / null space — what a projection keeps and what it kills.
  • interactiveImmersive Linear Algebra — orthogonal projection (Ch. 3)~30 min Interactive projection onto lines and planes.

Centroids, centering, covariance + eigendecomposition

Condition centroids are the repo's unit of geometry; PCA is eigendecomposition of the covariance matrix. Center first, or your cosine measures where the cloud sits, not the shift. → analysis/geometry.py
  • video3B1B — "Eigenvectors and eigenvalues" (Ch. 14)~17 min Why eigenvectors are the directions a transformation can't rotate — the heart of PCA.
  • videoStatQuest — "Covariance, Clearly Explained"~22 min What the covariance matrix actually encodes, one axis pair at a time.
  • textMML book — Ch. 4.2–4.4 (eigen, diagonalization, spectral)~40 pp The rigorous backstop once the pictures land.

SVD + PCA

The reduction step of the persistence test. SVD is the machinery under sklearn's PCA and under low-rank structure generally. → the dimension-reduction leg of the shift-vector experiment
  • videoBrunton — SVD series (start: first 3 videos, then the PCA ones)~1.5 h of the 7 h SVD → PCA connection made explicit, with Python code you can port.
  • textMML book — Ch. 10 (PCA as projection / low-rank approximation)~25 pp PCA three ways: variance-max, projection, reconstruction — all the same thing.

High-dimensional weirdness (concentration of measure)

The #1 out-of-ass vaccine. In D=1024 (WavLM/DAC), two random vectors sit at cos ≈ 0 ± 1/√D — so cos 0.3 across clips is enormous there and weak in D=3. Judge every Δ-concentration claim against this null. → any cosine you ever report
  • video"Curse of Dimensionality, Easily Explained"~10 min Volume growth, sparsity, distance concentration — the intuition breakdown, visualized.
  • textMML book — high-dim remarks in Ch. 10 + the 1/√D null (compute it yourself: sample pairs of np.random.randn(1024) and histogram the cosines)10 min of numpy Ten lines of numpy make this permanent better than any video.

Tier 2 — the measurement layer

What the probes and gates actually compute.

Least squares & ridge regression

The severity probe and the quality head are literally ridge: w = (XᵀX + λI)⁻¹Xᵀy. λ is why it survives D > n. → probes/severity.py, quality/head.py
  • videoKhan Academy — "Least squares approximation"~9 min Least squares as projection — ties Tier 1 directly to regression.
  • videoBrunton — "Least Squares and the SVD" (in the SVD series)~30 min Pseudoinverse + why regularization tames ill-conditioning.
  • video"Ridge vs Lasso Regression, Visualized"~12 min Why ridge shrinks and lasso zeros — the penalty geometry.

Logistic regression / softmax, hyperplane boundaries

The type probe. "Linearly readable" = a hyperplane separates it; consistent Δ direction ⇒ that hyperplane exists. → probes/type_probe.py
  • videoStatQuest — "Logistic Regression"~26 min S-curve, max likelihood, and the decision boundary as a line/plane.
  • videoStatQuest — "ArgMax and SoftMax" (NN playlist)~8 min Two classes → seven families: softmax is the multiclass extension the type probe uses.

Standardization, whitening, Mahalanobis distance

Raw-space and standardized-space cosines disagree — the additivity test reports both on purpose. Mahalanobis = Euclidean after whitening: distance in units of natural variation. → combos/additivity.py
  • video"Whitening transformation" lecture~10 min PCA-whitening / sphering — what "removing the covariance" means geometrically.
  • textscikit-learn — "Robust covariance estimation and Mahalanobis distances"short + runnable Concrete Python; the ellipse pictures do the teaching.

Spearman vs Pearson correlation

Gate 1 keys on Spearman SRCC (rank/monotone), not Pearson (linear) — monotonicity is the weaker, more honest claim. → probes/metrics.py
  • videoStatQuest — "Pearson's Correlation"~13 min Correlation as normalized covariance — the r you already know, properly.
  • video"Pearson vs Spearman — when to use which"~8 min Rank-based vs linear, outlier robustness — exactly the Gate 1 design choice.

Bootstrap & confidence intervals

Gates compare CI-lower vs CI-upper, resampling over test groups (speakers), not clips — clips from one speaker aren't independent. That one word is the difference between a result and a leak. → probes/metrics.py
  • videoStatQuest — "Confidence Intervals, Clearly Explained"~18 min What a CI is and isn't — before you read a single gate report.
  • videoStatQuest — "Bootstrap resampling"~14 min Resample-with-replacement intuition; mentally swap "sample" for "speaker group".

Tier 3 — the differentiable-metric layer

Where this is going: gradients through the encoder, and what the metric can't see.

Gradients, Jacobian, chain rule / backprop

"Differentiable metric" = ∂loss/∂audio flows through the encoder. The chain rule is backprop. → the endgame
  • video3B1B — "Visualizing the chain rule and product rule" (Essence of Calculus Ch. 4)~13 min The nested-machines picture of the chain rule.
  • video3B1B — "Backpropagation calculus" (NN series Ch. 4)~10 min Chain rule scaled up to networks — gradient flow made visible.
  • textParr & Howard — "The Matrix Calculus You Need for Deep Learning"~70 pp, skimmable Jacobians without tears; the reference when notation fights you.

Gradient descent & loss surfaces

What "the generator optimizes against the metric" means — and why a metric with bad gradients gets gamed. → why Gate 2 exists
  • video3B1B — "Gradient descent, how neural networks learn" (NN Ch. 2)~17 min The rolling-downhill loss landscape, animated.
  • videoStatQuest — "Stochastic Gradient Descent"~9 min Mini-batch vs full-batch — the practical texture of training.

Manifolds + why t-SNE/UMAP distort global structure

Encoders have non-trivial null spaces (what the metric can't see lives there), and latent data sits on manifolds — so nonlinear embeddings fake global distances by design. Reduce to visualize, never to measure. → the reduction-persistence test
  • interactiveDistill — "How to Use t-SNE Effectively"~15 min The canonical demo of cluster sizes and distances meaning nothing in t-SNE. Play with every widget.
  • video"Nonlinear manifold learning with t-SNE and UMAP"~25 min Side-by-side local-vs-global preservation — the exact caveat for the persistence test.
